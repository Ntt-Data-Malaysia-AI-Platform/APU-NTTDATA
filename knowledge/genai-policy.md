## Principles
APU encourages responsible use of generative AI tools to support learning. Students remain responsible for the work they submit, and AI use must be honest, acknowledged and allowed by the assessment brief.

## What students may do
Unless the assessment brief says otherwise, students may use GenAI tools to: explain concepts, generate practice questions, get feedback on grammar and structure, and brainstorm ideas. Students must acknowledge any GenAI use in a short statement at the end of the submission, naming the tool and how it was used.

## What students may not do
Students may not submit AI-generated text, code, images or analysis as their own work, use GenAI in assessments marked "No AI", or use GenAI to fabricate data, references or citations. Doing so is treated as academic misconduct under the Academic Integrity Policy.

## Guidance for lecturers
Each assessment brief must state one of three levels: "No AI", "AI-assisted" (allowed with acknowledgement) or "AI-integrated" (AI use is part of the task). Lecturers should design assessments that value process, reflection and in-class components. AI detection tools must not be the sole evidence for a misconduct case. Lecturers keep the final decision on every mark; AI-drafted feedback must be reviewed before release.
